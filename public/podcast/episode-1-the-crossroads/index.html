Episode 1: The Crossroads

August 16, 2026

Recording not yet published.

This is The Audio Blog, episode one. I want to explain quickly what this is before I get into anything, because there's no back catalog to point at yet. This isn't me reading blog posts out loud. It's a separate thing, recorded, less edited, how I actually talk when I'm being honest with myself. Some weeks it'll follow up on something I wrote. Some weeks it'll be the only place a thing gets said at all.

Before anything else, if this is the first time you're hearing my voice: I'm Ayoola Morakinyo. I build software, I'm self-taught in machine learning, and right now I'm running two startups, Square A and MDFLD, out of one seat. That's who's talking.

Today it's a follow-up. I put up a post earlier called "The Problem I Still Think Is Mine to Solve," and I want to go further on it here, because writing it down made me careful in a way I don't want to be careful right now.

Here's the short version if you haven't read it. I run two startups. Square A is moving. MDFLD isn't, not right now, and MDFLD is the one I actually care about most. That's a strange sentence to sit with. The thing getting the least of me is the thing I'd probably still be trying to solve even if I ended up doing something else entirely.

I want to say more about why, because in the post I kept it pretty abstract. Trust, honesty, your boots and kit being what they claim to be. Here's the less polished version. I grew up around football. I know what it feels like to want a pair of boots or a piece of kit because of what it represents, and I know what it feels like to find out later that what you got wasn't real. That specific kind of letdown, being sold a story instead of a fact, it bothers me more than it probably should. I don't think that's a market opportunity I identified. I think it's just a thing I noticed I care about, and then I built a company around it because that's the tool I had.

So when I say it feels like mine to solve, I mean that literally. Not mine like I own the idea. Mine like if I stopped working on MDFLD tomorrow, I'd still be annoyed at counterfeit boots five years from now, wherever I was.

Okay. The harder part.

Right now I'm living at my parents' house. Free rent, free food, and I want to say clearly that this is not a small thing. It's the only reason any of this is possible at all. Neither startup really pays me. If I were paying my own way right now, I don't think I'd have the runway to keep MDFLD alive at the pace I'm keeping it alive, which honestly isn't much of a pace. So there's real gratitude there, and I don't say it enough.

But I also think about the roof having an end date. Not because anyone's kicking me out. Just because that's not a forever arrangement, and some part of me is always doing the math on how much longer I get to build like this before something has to change.

If I could ask God to answer one prayer right now, it's not a funding round, even though obviously that would help. It's just being able to afford to work on MDFLD, full time, without needing something else underneath it. That's it. That's the whole prayer. It's smaller than people probably expect from someone talking about a prayer getting answered.

Now the part that's newer, the part I don't think I've said publicly before this.

I've never had a structured environment to actually get good at the technical core of what MDFLD needs, which is fine-grained visual classification. Telling a real thing from a very good fake, at the level of detail that matters, in an image. Everything I know about that I taught myself because I had to, building the thing under pressure, no formal path through it. And I've hit the edge of what teaching myself under pressure can get me to. I want to learn from people who are meaningfully further along than I am, and here's the thing I've realized: that doesn't have to happen in one specific place. It could be grad school, sitting in a room with people who've spent years on exactly this. It could just as easily be hiring the best people in this field into MDFLD itself, once I can, and learning by working next to them every single day. Both get me the same thing, closer to people who know more than I do. I'm not married to one door for that. I want whichever one actually gets me there, or maybe both, eventually.

Which is why I've got two Notion boards open right now. One's jobs. One's grad school. I'm working both, seriously, at the same time I'm still pushing whatever hours I can find for MDFLD. If that sounds unresolved, it's because it is. I don't have the ending yet. I'm not going to fake one for the sake of a clean close to this episode.

What I do know, and this is the only thing I actually know for sure, is that the problem doesn't disappear no matter which door I walk through. Job, grad school, building the team out myself once I can, none of it changes what I think is actually mine to figure out. The rest is logistics I'm still working out in real time, on two Notion boards, out loud, starting here.

That's episode one. If something ships, or breaks, or gets resolved, you'll hear about it here before it's tidy enough to write down.
