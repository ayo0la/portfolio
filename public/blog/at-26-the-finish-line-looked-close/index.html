At 26, the Finish Line Looked Close

September 20, 2026

On Wednesday the punch of reality caught me square in the stomach.

A friend I have had since I was twelve, thirteen years of friendship, told me he is seriously ill. He has been in treatment for months. He carried it quietly all that while, and when he finally told me, he ended the message by thanking me. Thanking me! The man is in the fight of his life and he is the one handing out gratitude.

I will say no more about him than that. It is his story, and he is still in the thick of it. What I can tell you is what it did to my week, because it did plenty.

Thursday

On Thursday I called him. The calls didn't even go through. Just dead air, the silence of a phone switched off, or of a man going through hell.

With the phone still in my hand and nothing on the other end of it, my mind wandered down the alleys we warn children never to enter, and it did not stop until it reached the worst thought of all: the finish line.

No one truly wants to see the finish line in their race of life, least of all when the race has barely started. He is 26. I am 25. At our age you are supposed to be arguing about pace, not distance. And yet there it was, for one long evening, close enough to read the banner.

What do you do with a thought like that? I did the only two useful things I know. I prayed for him. And I promised to check on him far more often than I have been, which, if I am honest, was not nearly often enough.

What I wrote on Monday

The strange part is that I had already written about all of this before I knew a thing.

I keep a journal at the end of most days. Monday was a day I did not enjoy, and still I wrote that I find tremendous joy within my days, that I take them one at a time like anyone can, and that I give my best regardless, because today might be my last.

I meant it. But I meant it the way you mean things that are true in general, the way you agree that the ocean is deep while standing dry on the beach. By Wednesday night I was in the water. The thought had a face, and it was a face I have known for thirteen years.

Monday was also the day I met a friend's newborn son for the first time. He shares my middle name. For about four glorious seconds I believed he had been named after me. He was not, loool. Still, I carried him around in my head all week. A start line and a finish line, shown to me within two days of each other, and me somewhere on the track between them with no idea what lap I am on. Neither do you, by the way.

Too relaxed for my own good

A month ago I wrote in a post here that I am usually too relaxed about time for my own good. I said it like a charming quirk. This week it read like a confession.

A mentor told me recently that I get stuck in overanalysis and then produce nothing. He was not being cruel. He was being accurate. I can spend a week studying the course for a race that would have taken an afternoon to simply run, badly, and then run better the next day.

So here is how the week actually went once the news had settled.

MDFLD posted on Instagram for the first time in about three months. Three months! For a company whose entire near-term plan is to show up every single day, that is a striker forgetting where the goal is. My cofounder and I have now agreed to post multiple times a day, every day, and for the first time in a long while there is urgency in how the two of us move.

I had an interview, and the wind is in my sails, for I am through to the next round. My next opponent is the hiring manager. This is war, and if I am to obtain victory I must be well prepared. Prepare I shall.

I kept at the boot model database, the one I have been building for a really, really, really long time. It is close now. It is the quiet engine beneath everything MDFLD does, and I have stopped treating "close" as a place I am allowed to live.

And I said a hard thing to someone I have known most of my life, a thing I had been putting off for far too long. I do not yet know how that one ends. I know I would rather have said it than still be waiting for a better moment.

I also made peace with the pace of this blog. I set out to write here every week, which was ambitious, and the gaps between posts have told the truth on me. From here on it will be fortnightly. A pace I can hold beats a sprint I cannot, and I would rather keep a promise every two weeks than break one every week.

None of this is heroic. It is simply what happens when "later" stops feeling like a guarantee.

This is not a sad post

Be careful with urgency. Left alone it curdles into fear, and fear is not what I felt.

This same week I finally graced the fields of Georgia Tech with my presence. I tend to avoid the place because of the barbarians who roam the vast, rugged lands of Roe Stamps, and they did not disappoint. It was as though Artemis and Apollo had turned up with the armies they command, and brought friends. I was, of course, victorious. My back, however, has lodged a formal complaint and remains very sore.

I would go back tomorrow. I never have more fun in my life than when I am near that intoxicating truncated icosahedron. The ball was my first friend, my best friend, and my first love.

The week also held a movie night with my family, which is always currency well spent. We watched the making of Ray Kroc, the "founder" of McDonald's, and the lesson went down like an ice cold flat Coke: determination, persistence, and then more persistence.

And it held Shoe Dog, read with my eyes and, whenever my eyes were busy, with my ears. Phil Knight says that play is what we all dream life could be. For me, work and play are one and the same, because my play is competition, and I dearly love to find out who comes out on top in the end.

Knight is very much alive, but most of my teachers are not. Becoming friends with the eminent dead has been one of the best decisions of my life. Long before this blog was born, I was reading about people long gone, founders mostly, and what strikes me is that not one of them knew what lap they were on while they were running it. They simply kept running, with more persistence than seemed reasonable.

So the feeling this week was never "hurry, you are running out of time." It was closer to "this is already the good part, so stop saving it for later."

If there is someone you keep meaning to call, call them this week. If the call does not go through, call again tomorrow. That is my plan too.

Somewhere tonight a boy with my middle name is at the start line, all of it ahead of him. Somewhere my friend is fighting for every step of his next lap. And I am on the track between them, 25 years old, with no idea how far off the banner is.

So I will run.
