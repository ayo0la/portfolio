---
title: "The Problem I Still Think Is Mine to Solve"
date: 2026-08-16
tldr: "Between two startups, the one whose problem I love most is the one I have left on the bench. Some honesty about why, and about the crossroads I am actually standing at."
---

Every manager has a player he believes in most. Mine is the one I keep benching.

I run two startups right now, and MDFLD is the one getting neglected. Not because I care about it less. If anything it is the opposite, and that is the part that is hard to admit.

I love the problem MDFLD is trying to solve. Trust in what you are actually buying, your boots and kit being what they claim to be, a layer of honesty in a market that runs on people not knowing what they are getting. It is the kind of problem I think I would still be chasing even if I ended up somewhere else entirely, a different job, a different startup that is not mine. Less a business idea I am testing, more something that is actually mine to solve, whether or not MDFLD the company is the vehicle for it.

And it is the one I have been giving the fewest minutes to.

What kind of manager does that? Part of it is bandwidth. Two startups from one seat means something always loses. The rest is more honest than that: traction is slow right now, funding has not landed, and it is discouraging in a way that is easier to route around by working on the thing that is currently moving instead of the thing I actually care about most. I do not love writing that down. It is true anyway.

Let me be clear about what this is not. I am not losing patience with the idea, and I am not panicking about timing. If anything I am someone who is usually too relaxed about time for my own good. What is actually thinning is the ambient discouragement, the gap between how much I believe in the problem and how little visible progress there has been lately, and I feel that gap more some weeks than others.

Here is a thing I do not say enough: I am living at my parents' house right now, for free, and that is the entire reason any of this is possible. If I could wave a magic wand and get one thing, it would not be a funding round or a growth hack. It would be being able to afford to work on MDFLD full time, on my own, without needing anything else to pay the bills first. For the roof I am grateful. I am also honest that the roof is a runway with an end on it, and I think about that.

Which is where the other half of this gets honest too. Never have I been in a structured environment to go deep on the specific technical problem underneath MDFLD, fine-grained visual classification, telling real from fake at the level of detail that matters. Everything I know about it I taught myself, out of necessity, building the thing, the way a street player learns with no academy and no coach. I want more than that. I want to learn from people who know meaningfully more than I do, in a setting built for exactly that kind of depth.

So two Notion boards sit open. One is jobs. One is grad school. I am applying to both, seriously, at the same time I keep building MDFLD in whatever hours are left. A clean answer for where this goes, I do not have, nor will I pretend to.

What I do know is this. Whatever the boards decide, whatever league I end up playing in, whichever team pays my bills, the problem does not go away for me. That part I am sure of. The rest, I am figuring out in public, starting with this post.

The player is still on my team sheet. Benched for now, and absolutely not for sale.
