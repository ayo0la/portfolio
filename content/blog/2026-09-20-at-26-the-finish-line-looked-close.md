On Wednesday I found out that a friend I've had since I was twelve is seriously ill. He has been in treatment for months. I only learned about it this week, in a message that ended with him thanking me.

I'm not going to say more about him than that. It's his story, and he's still in the middle of it. This post is about what it did to my week, because it did something.

## Thursday

I tried to call him on Thursday. The calls didn't go through. Maybe his phone was off. Maybe he was in treatment and having the kind of day I can't picture.

My mind went down the alleys we warn kids not to wander into, and it stopped at the worst thought, the finish line. Nobody wants to see the finish line in their race, especially when the race has barely started. And yet here we are, at 26, and for an evening it looked close.

I prayed for him. I promised myself I'd check on him more often than I have been. Those are the only two useful things I've figured out how to do so far.

## The thing I wrote on Monday

What's strange is that I had already written about this before I knew anything.

I keep a journal at the end of most days. Monday was a day I did not enjoy. I wrote that I still find tremendous joy inside my days, that I take them one at a time, and that I give my best anyway, because today might be my last one.

I meant it when I wrote it. But I meant it the way you mean things that are true in general. By Wednesday night it wasn't general anymore. It had a face, and it was a face I've known for thirteen years.

Monday was also the day I met a friend's newborn son for the first time. He happens to share my middle name. For about four seconds I thought he'd been named after me. He wasn't, haha. But I held onto that all week. A beginning and a finish line showed up within two days of each other, and I'm somewhere between them with no idea how far along I am. Neither do you, by the way.

## What I've been relaxed about

A month ago I wrote [in a post here](/blog/mine-to-solve/) that I'm usually too relaxed about time for my own good. I said it like a personality quirk. This week it read more like a confession.

A mentor told me recently that I get stuck in overanalysis and then produce nothing. He wasn't being mean. He was being accurate. I can spend a week building the perfect plan for a thing that would have taken an afternoon to just do, badly, and then do better the next day.

So here is what the week actually looked like once the news settled in.

MDFLD posted on Instagram for the first time in about three months. Three months. For a company whose whole near-term plan is showing up every day. My cofounder and I agreed to post multiple times a day, every day, and for the first time in a while there's real urgency in how the two of us are moving.

I had an interview that went well, and I prepared for the next round like it was a fight, because it is one.

I kept pushing on the boot model database I've been building for what feels like forever. It's close now. It's the quiet thing underneath everything else MDFLD does, and I've stopped treating "close" as a place I'm allowed to live.

And I said something hard to someone I've known most of my life, something I'd been putting off for a long time. I don't know yet how that one ends. I know I'd rather have said it than still be waiting for a better moment.

None of that is heroic. It's just what happens when "later" stops feeling like a guarantee.

## This isn't a sad post

I want to be careful here, because urgency can turn into a kind of fear, and that's not what I felt.

The same week had pickup football at Georgia Tech, where I got properly beaten up and won anyway, and my back is still reminding me. It had a movie night with my family, which is always currency well spent. It had Shoe Dog in my ears whenever my eyes were busy. Phil Knight says somewhere in there that play is what we all dream life could be. I think work and play are the same thing when you love the work, and I love the work. Football was my first friend. Building something around it is as close to play as a job gets.

So the feeling wasn't "hurry, you're running out of time." It was closer to "this is already the good part, so stop saving it for later."

Long before the birth of this blog, I've been reading about people who are long gone, founders mostly, and what strikes me is how none of them knew which chapter they were in while they were in it. They just kept going, with more persistence than seemed reasonable. I don't know what chapter this is for me either. I know my friend didn't get to choose his.

If you've got someone you keep meaning to call, call them this week. If the call doesn't go through, try again the next day. Well at least, that's my whole plan too.
